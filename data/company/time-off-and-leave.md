# Time Off and Leave

## What is the vacation policy?

Full-time employees accrue paid vacation days starting from their first day, at a rate specified in your offer letter (commonly 20-25 days per year depending on role and location, with some regions having statutory minimums that apply automatically). Accrued days can be viewed in the HR portal under "Time Off," updated after each pay cycle.

## Can I take vacation before I've accrued any days?

Some companies allow negative balance/advance use of vacation days within policy limits, typically up to 5 days in advance of accrual, subject to manager approval. Check your specific offer letter or the time-off policy document in the HR portal for the exact advance-use limit that applies to your role and location.

## How do I request time off?

Submit a request through the HR portal's Time Off tool, specifying the dates and (optionally) a reason. Your manager receives an approval request and typically responds within 2 business days. For anything longer than a week, it's good practice to also give your manager a heads-up in person or via message before submitting the formal request.

## What is the sick leave policy?

Sick leave is generally separate from vacation days and doesn't require advance notice — just notify your manager as early as possible on the day you're out. Most roles have either unlimited reasonable sick leave (with manager discretion) or a set statutory allowance depending on your country; specifics are in the time-off policy document. For absences longer than 3 consecutive days, a doctor's note may be required per local regulation.

## What parental leave is offered?

Parental leave policies vary significantly by country due to differing statutory requirements, but the company provides leave beyond statutory minimums in most locations — typically including paid leave for the primary caregiver and a shorter paid leave for the secondary caregiver, plus the option for additional unpaid leave. Full details, including how leave interacts with statutory maternity/paternity leave in your country, are in the Family and Dependents document, and the Benefits Team can walk you through your specific situation.

## How much notice do I need to give for planned vacation?

For standard vacation requests, 2 weeks' notice is the general expectation so your manager and team can plan around your absence, though this isn't a hard rule for shorter absences. For extended leave (2+ weeks), give as much notice as possible, ideally 4-6 weeks, to allow for proper handoff planning.

## Do unused vacation days roll over to the next year?

This depends on your country's regulations and the specific policy — some locations allow a capped rollover (e.g. up to 5 days into the new year), while others require use-it-or-lose-it by year end, and some pay out unused days. Check the time-off policy document for your specific location's rule, since this is one of the areas with the most regional variation.

## What if I need to take an extended personal leave of absence?

Personal leave beyond standard vacation/sick time is handled case by case — reach out to HR directly to discuss your situation. Depending on the reason and duration, this may be unpaid, may affect benefits continuation (see the Healthcare and Insurance document), and requires manager and HR sign-off before it's finalized.

## What company holidays are observed?

The list of paid company holidays is specific to your country and office location (since public holidays differ significantly by region) and is published in the HR portal under "Time Off Policy" — this is updated annually and includes both statutory public holidays and any additional company-designated days off.

## What happens to unused vacation time if I leave the company?

Payout of unused vacation upon departure is governed by local law and company policy, and varies by country — in many jurisdictions, unused accrued vacation must legally be paid out; in others, it depends on company policy. HR will confirm your specific payout (if any) as part of the offboarding process.
