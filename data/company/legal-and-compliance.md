# Legal and Compliance

## Can I negotiate my start date after signing my contract?

Contact your recruiter directly if you need to adjust your start date after signing. Changes are handled case by case depending on business need and how much notice you can give — reach out as early as possible once you know a change is needed, since some pre-start logistics (equipment shipping, account provisioning) are timed against your original date.

## What happens if I need to cancel or postpone my start?

Reach out to your recruiter as soon as possible to discuss your situation. This is handled individually, confidentially, and won't negatively affect future opportunities with the company. The sooner you communicate, the more flexibility there generally is to work something out.

## Do I need a work permit or visa?

This depends entirely on your citizenship and the country you'll be working in. If your role requires visa sponsorship, this is typically handled during the offer stage, not after signing — if you're unsure whether your situation requires anything further, contact the Legal/Immigration team listed in your offer documentation as soon as possible, since visa processing timelines can affect your actual start date.

## What does the IP assignment clause in my contract mean?

Standard employment contracts include an intellectual property (IP) assignment clause, meaning work products, inventions, and code you create within the scope of your employment belong to the company. This is standard across the industry and doesn't typically extend to unrelated personal projects done on your own time with your own equipment, though exact scope depends on your specific contract language — read the clause carefully and raise questions with Legal before your start date if anything is unclear.

## How does the company handle my personal data (GDPR/privacy)?

The company processes your personal data (contact details, banking information, tax documents, etc.) strictly for employment-related purposes, in compliance with applicable data protection law (including GDPR for EU-based employees). You have the right to request access to, correction of, or deletion of your personal data, subject to legal retention requirements — the full privacy policy is available in the HR portal, and you can direct specific questions to privacy@company.com.

## What is a non-compete or non-solicitation clause, and does mine have one?

Some contracts include non-compete (restricting work for competitors for a period after leaving) or non-solicitation (restricting recruiting former colleagues or clients) clauses. Whether these apply to you, and their exact scope and duration, depends on your role, seniority, and the jurisdiction you're employed in — some regions restrict or prohibit non-competes entirely by law. Review your specific contract, and Legal can clarify anything unclear before you sign or during onboarding.

## What is the probationary period, and what does it mean for me?

Many contracts include a probationary period (commonly 3-6 months depending on country and role) during which either party can end employment with shorter notice than the standard terms. This is standard practice, not a signal of concern about your performance specifically — it's a mutual trial period. Your specific probation length and notice terms are in your contract.

## Who do I contact with a legal question about my contract?

For contract interpretation questions, reach out to legal@company.com, or in some cases your recruiter can route the question appropriately. For anything sensitive or personal, you can also request a confidential conversation rather than putting details in writing initially.

## What confidentiality obligations do I have?

Standard confidentiality clauses require you to protect company trade secrets, client information, and non-public business information both during and after employment. This is separate from your public communications (like normal social media use) but does restrict sharing internal company information externally. Full details are in your contract's confidentiality section.
