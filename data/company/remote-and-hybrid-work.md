# Remote and Hybrid Work

## Can I work remotely during my first week?

This depends on your role and team's specific policy. Most roles require at least the first day on-site for setup, badge issuance, and in-person introductions, even if the role is otherwise hybrid or remote long-term. Check with your manager in your welcome email for the exact expectation for your first week.

## What equipment do I get for a home office?

IT ships a monitor, keyboard, mouse, and webcam along with your laptop if your role is hybrid or remote, at no cost to you. Request additional equipment (a second monitor, an ergonomic chair, a headset) through the IT equipment request portal — reasonable requests are generally approved without much friction.

## Is there a home office stipend?

Yes, most fully remote and hybrid roles receive a one-time home office setup stipend (amount varies by country and role, detailed in your offer letter) plus an optional recurring monthly stipend to cover a portion of internet/utility costs. Check your specific offer letter or the remote work policy document for the exact amounts applicable to you.

## What are the core working hours for remote employees?

Most teams operate with a set of "core hours" (commonly late morning to mid-afternoon in your team's primary timezone) during which you're expected to be reachable for meetings and collaboration, with flexibility outside that window. Your manager will confirm your team's specific core hours and any timezone-overlap expectations during your first week.

## How often do hybrid employees need to come into the office?

This varies by team — some teams have a fixed number of required in-office days per week (commonly 2-3), while others are more flexible based on project needs. Your manager will confirm your team's specific hybrid schedule expectation, which is typically outlined in your welcome email or discussed in your first week.

## Can I work from a different country temporarily?

This requires advance approval from HR and sometimes Legal, due to tax and employment law implications of working across borders, even temporarily. Don't assume this is automatically fine — submit a request through the remote work policy portal well in advance (commonly 4+ weeks) of any planned international work period.

## What if my internet or home setup fails during work hours?

Notify your manager as soon as it happens — this is treated the same as any other unexpected disruption, not something requiring special justification. If you have access to a nearby coworking space or the office, that's a reasonable fallback; otherwise, just communicate the situation and adjust your availability as needed.

## How do remote employees stay connected with their team?

Most teams use a mix of regular video check-ins, an active team chat channel, and periodic in-person gatherings (varies by team and budget) to maintain connection despite distributed work. Your manager and buddy will help you get oriented into your specific team's communication norms during your first week.

## Do remote employees get the same benefits as office-based employees?

Yes, benefits eligibility is based on employment status (full-time/part-time), not work location — remote employees receive the same health insurance, PTO, and other benefits as office-based peers, plus the added home office stipend that office-based employees don't receive.

## What security requirements apply to working from home?

The same endpoint security software required on any company device applies at home, plus you're expected to use a private, password-protected WiFi network (not public WiFi) for work, and the VPN is required when accessing certain internal systems remotely. Full details are in the IT and Security document.
