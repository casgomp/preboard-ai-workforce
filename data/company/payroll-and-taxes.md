# Payroll and Taxes

## When will I receive my first paycheck?

Payroll runs monthly, paid on the last business day of each month. If you start mid-month, your first payment covers a prorated amount for the days worked and arrives on the next standard payroll date — you are not paid retroactively on a special schedule, so budget for this if your start date falls after the 1st. Your exact prorated amount will be visible on your first payslip.

## How do I set up direct deposit?

You'll complete a banking details form as part of your digital onboarding paperwork, sent roughly 10 days before your start date. You'll need your bank's routing/sort code and your account number. If this form isn't submitted before your first payroll cutoff, your first payment will be issued as a physical check instead, mailed to your address on file, which takes longer — so submitting it early is worth doing.

## What tax forms do I need to submit?

Required tax forms vary by country and are pre-loaded into your onboarding checklist based on the location listed on your contract. In the US, this typically includes a W-4 for federal withholding and any applicable state tax forms. In the EU, this typically includes your tax ID / tax class documentation. All forms are completed digitally through the onboarding portal — you do not need to mail anything.

## How do I read my payslip?

Your payslip breaks down gross pay, all deductions (taxes, benefits premiums, retirement contributions), and net pay. It's available digitally in the payroll portal on payday, typically a few days before funds actually arrive in your account. A guide explaining each line item is linked from the payslip portal the first time you log in.

## What if my bank details or personal information change?

Update your banking details or address directly in the payroll portal — changes take effect for the next payroll cycle, not the current one, so make updates as early as possible in the month to avoid a delayed or misdirected payment. For name changes (e.g. after marriage), you'll also need to update your legal documentation with HR alongside the payroll system.

## Am I paid overtime?

This depends on your role classification, which is specified in your contract. Non-exempt roles are eligible for overtime pay at the statutory or company-defined rate for hours worked beyond the standard threshold; exempt roles (typically salaried professional and management positions) are not eligible for overtime pay under standard policy. Your offer letter specifies which classification applies to you.

## How does the annual bonus work?

Eligible roles receive an annual performance bonus, paid out following the year-end review cycle, typically in Q1 of the following year. Bonus eligibility, target percentage, and whether you qualify in your first partial year (often prorated based on start date) are detailed in your offer letter — check there first, and reach out to Payroll if anything is unclear.

## What deductions come out of my paycheck automatically?

Standard deductions include income tax withholding, statutory social security/national insurance contributions, and your elected benefits premiums (health, dental, vision) if you're enrolled. If you opted into the retirement plan or HSA with employee contributions, those are also deducted pre-tax. Your first payslip will show the exact breakdown once your benefit elections are finalized.

## Who do I contact about a payroll error?

Email payroll@company.com with your employee ID and a description of the discrepancy. Payroll issues are prioritized and typically investigated within 2 business days; if it's a significant shortfall affecting your ability to cover expenses, flag this explicitly in your message and the team will expedite a correction.

## Do I get paid during company holidays?

Yes, all company-designated holidays are paid for eligible employees, with no impact on your salary or hourly pay. The list of observed holidays for your country/office location is in the time-off policy, available in the HR portal.
